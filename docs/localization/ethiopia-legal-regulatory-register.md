# Ethiopia Legal and Regulatory Source Register — Apparel, Finance, Procurement and Workforce

**Research baseline date:** 22 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Primary-source register for the Ethiopia localization pack of Modern Garment End-to-End Addis ERP.  
**Important:** This is an engineering source register, not a legal opinion or an assertion that every implementing directive has already been fully interpreted. No statutory rate or obligation becomes executable until the complete current text, applicability, effective date and local professional approval are recorded.

## 1. Regulatory operating rule

All fiscal, payroll, procurement, commercial, import/export, finance, workforce, privacy, environmental and product-compliance rules must be sourced sector by sector from the authority that owns or administers that subject.

Use the Ministry of Justice / Federal Negarit Gazette as the legal-publication and cross-sector discovery index; use the relevant issuing authority for current implementation directives, forms, e-services, notices and procedural updates.

Never use an ERP vendor blog, accountant blog, sample repository, old spreadsheet or copied tax table as the authoritative source of a legal obligation. Secondary material may help interpret a provision, but the rule must link to the primary authority and Gazette/directive.

### Regulatory source-of-truth hierarchy
1. Federal Negarit Gazette publication of the proclamation, regulation or amendment.
2. Current official directive issued by the competent authority.
3. Official implementation circular, notice, tax calendar, form, ruling or e-service guidance from the authority.
4. Regional/state or city authority law and guidance where the taxpayer, employer, site or transaction is regionally administered.
5. Written opinion/approval by the customer's qualified Ethiopian lawyer, accountant, tax practitioner, customs specialist or payroll professional.
6. Industry standards and good practices are recorded separately; they never override local law.

## 4. Mandatory rule registry structure in the ERP

Every legal/tax/payroll/procurement rule is a versioned record with:

- Authority, jurisdiction, legal instrument type, number, title, subject, official source URL, Gazette issue/page and exact document file/hash.
- Publication date, commencement/effective date, transition period, effective-from/effective-to, repeals/amendments/supersession relationships.
- Scope/applicability: entity type, legal activity, taxpayer/worker category, region/city, industry, transaction type, product, contract, site and channel.
- Original legal clause reference and a plain-language interpretation separately stored; keep Amharic and English source variants when available.
- Rule parameters in structured form (rates, thresholds, bases, calendars, filing deadlines, forms, evidence types, exemptions) with decimal/date precision and no hidden hard-coded constants.
- Source status: discovered, downloaded, text-extracted, reviewed, translated, professionally interpreted, approved for configuration, tested, active, superseded or withdrawn.
- Reviewer identity/role, local tax/legal/payroll sign-off, approval date, unit tests, sample calculations, edge cases and release version.
- Historical reproduction: every posted tax return, payroll, invoice, customs declaration and compliance decision points to the exact legal-rule versions and source documents used.
- Change impact mapping to modules, API contracts, report templates, open transactions and future due dates.

## 5. Legal change-management workflow

1. Monitor Ministry of Justice Gazette/law/directive listings and each sector authority's official website/feed.
2. Capture new or amended instrument with source URL, original PDF, publication metadata and checksum.
3. Compare with the prior version and mark changed, added, repealed, transitional and uncertain clauses.
4. Map obligations to tax, payroll, procurement/AP, AR/POS, import/export, HR, environmental, customer/consumer and financial-reporting workflows.
5. Obtain professional review for interpretation, effective date, affected population and transition treatment.
6. Prepare rule configuration in a draft state with test vectors; do not change historical posted transactions.
7. Run parallel calculation, regression and reconciliation tests; review differences and unresolved cases.
8. Require authorized business/legal/accounting/payroll approval before activating the rule.
9. Apply to transactions in scope from the lawful effective date; preserve grandfathering/transitional treatment.
10. Archive superseded rules and keep full audit evidence.

A regulatory update must not rewrite prior approved payroll, tax returns, posted journals, stock valuation or customs documents. Corrections use lawful adjustment/reversal and amendment procedures.

## 6. Procurement law versus procurement best practice

The product has two distinct layers:

- **Legal applicability:** determine whether the legal entity is private, public enterprise, public body, government-funded/covered buyer or another statutorily covered institution. Apply the relevant public procurement act only if it is legally applicable.
- **Internal control standard:** regardless of legal status, support transparent supplier selection, approved vendor lists, competitive quotes, price reasonableness, conflicts declaration, separation of requester/buyer/receiver/payer, delegated approvals, budget commitment, contract control, three-way matching, duplicate invoice detection, supplier performance, audit history and whistleblowing/exception review.

The internal control layer is an enterprise best-practice baseline; it must not be described as a mandatory public tender law for private companies unless a specific rule applies.

## 7. Research boundary and open verification items

- **Digital-content creator business classification directive:** Article 22(3) of Proclamation No. 1395/2025 instructs the Tax Authority to issue a directive specifying when digital-content creation is treated as a business activity, considering income level, transaction type, content type and business purpose. That directive was not located in this official-source review. Regulation No. 597/2026, Articles 55–56 and 72–77, supplies Schedule C/Schedule D routes, business-creator records, TIN, a 15% Schedule D rate for resident creators not meeting the business test, an annual gross threshold above ETB 50,000, quarterly declaration/payment within 30 days, and platform reporting. The separate Article 22(3) directive requirement remains unresolved. Ambiguous cases must remain REVIEW REQUIRED.
- **Minimum alternative tax:** Proclamation No. 1395/2025 Article 23 and Regulation No. 597/2026 Articles 57–59 establish a 2.5% minimum alternative tax framework with taxpayer-specific bases, loss/nil-return estimation, credit and exemption conditions. Regulation 597 provides turnover basis for taxpayers declaring no taxable income/loss, and Tax Authority study-based estimation where no activity is declared. It is not a blanket 2.5% charge on every tenant or sale. Keep liability REVIEW REQUIRED until full law, applicability, transitions, credits and qualified tax sign-off are approved.

- Article 11 monthly employment-income table: ETB 0–2,000 0%; 2,001–4,000 15%; 4,001–7,000 20%; 7,001–10,000 25%; 10,001–14,000 30%; above ETB 14,000 35%. The table alone is not the complete progressive calculation engine; taxable base, deductions, exemptions and applicability require full-clause review.
- Article 4 effective dates: Alternative Minimum Tax applies to income tax collected from 8 July 2025; Schedule D income and withholding under renumbered Article 97 become effective 7 August 2025; other provisions apply to income derived from 8 July 2025, subject to the transition clause.
- Renumbered Article 97: 3% withholding is limited to specified withholding agents and covered domestic goods payments above ETB 20,000 in one transaction/supply contract and covered services involving ETB 10,000 in one supply contract. Special 30% withholding applies only where the supplier fails to provide TIN and business licence to the withholding agent for a transaction covered by Article 97(1). Neither rate is universal.
- Article 22 distinguishes creator business income, Schedule D income and a Tax Authority directive requirement. The specific Article 22(3) directive was not found in this review; ambiguous cases remain REVIEW REQUIRED.
- Article 23 MAT has different bases for ordinary bodies/persons, banks, insurers and price-regulated entities and includes reductions, exclusions, credits and exemptions. It is not a universal turnover tax.

### 8.2 Council of Ministers Federal Income Tax Regulation No. 597/2026
- Gazette publication: Federal Negarit Gazette 33rd Year No. 4, Addis Ababa, 28 October 2026. English title: Regulation No. 597/2026; the Ethiopian-calendar identifier is 597/2019 E.C.
- Official source: [Ministry of Justice Gazette PDF](https://justice.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/%E1%8C%98%E1%88%9B%E1%8C%AB%E1%88%AC-%E1%8C%A5%E1%88%AD-597-2019.pdf). The MoJ catalogue record is dated 6 November 2026, distinct from the Gazette issue date.
- The Regulation further amends Income Tax Regulation No. 410/2017. It aggregates connected/related-person projects for the 91-day permanent-establishment test; addresses specified resident digital/freelance/remote-service income; sets business-creator books/evidence duties for cash and in-kind income; distinguishes Schedule C from Schedule D; and provides resident Schedule D creators a 15% rate, gross annual threshold over ETB 50,000, quarterly declaration/payment within 30 days after each quarter (quarters commence 7 July each tax year), and quarterly platform/payment-facilitator reporting.
- MAT Articles 57–59 include a turnover basis for nil/loss declarations and Tax Authority study-based estimation where no activity is declared, together with credit carry-forward and exemption conditions. Apply the full clauses together.
- The final provision states the other provisions commence on Gazette publication, except a specified Article 78(1)(d) matter tied to a Ministry temporary decision. Use Gazette date, not catalogue date, for commencement.

### 8.4 Commercial registration and business licensing
The [Federal Negarit Gazette Proclamation No. 980/2016](https://www.motri.gov.et/sites/default/files/resource/proclamation-no-980_2016_0.pdf), Gazette No. 101 dated 5 August 2016, page 9212, Article 22, provides:
- 22(2): a valid licence holder need not obtain an additional business licence for same-activity branches. This does not remove Article 5(4) commercial-register branch registration and notification.
- 22(3): a manufacturing licensee may wholesale its own products only at the same address as its manufacturing facility or registered address without an additional wholesale licence.
- 22(4): a manufacturing licensee may not retail its products except product types permitted by Council of Ministers regulation.
- 22(5)–(6): licensed importer wholesale/retail treatment is separately governed; importer retail is also restricted except specified products under Council regulation.
The ERP must model legal entity + licence category + activity + site/address + branch-register entry + validity. A store/channel configuration must never itself grant legal authority to retail.

- **Pension:** POESSA Proclamation 1268/2022 includes the 45-day worker definition, employer 11%/employee 7% rates on the statutory salary base, and monthly remittance. Directives 1085/2025, 1086/2025 and 1087/2025 and current salary base/coverage/exceptions require local pension/payroll review.
- **Labour/OSH:** Labour Proclamation 1156/2019 and OSH Guide Directive 182/2013 are official starting sources. Capture later directives and region-specific practice; do not assume national samples are exhaustive.
- **Public procurement:** Proclamation 1333/2024 applies only where the entity is within statutory scope, including relevant public enterprises. Private-company sourcing controls are company policy/best practice, not public tender law.

- **Accounting:** Financial Reporting Proclamation 847/2014 and AABE standards/pronouncements; the reporting framework depends on entity/reporting class and current AABE adoption.
- **Privacy:** Personal Data Protection Proclamation 1321/2024; implementing authority, directions, cross-border transfers and data-subject processes require current legal review.
- **Environment/product standards:** Proclamations 300/2002 and 1090/2018, related industrial pollution provisions, local permits and current Ethiopian Standards Institute requirements must be resolved by process, chemical, product and site.
